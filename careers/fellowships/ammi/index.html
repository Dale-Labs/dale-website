APE / AMMI Fellow

What becomes legible when provenance remains connected across an entire market system?

AMMI asks: What happens if the whole story travels?

Opening problem

A product never arrives in a market alone.

It carries a place.

A history.

Knowledge.

Memory.

Methods of production.

Relationships.

Meaning.

But markets are very good at separating the thing being sold from the context that made it valuable.

Starting source

Culture, memory and context remain connected to provenance.

Q_P = (C_P, Π_P)

Culture / memory / contextual field + Provenance structure.

These must remain connected but not collapsed into one concept.

Starting model

Five hypothesised relations, each treated independently.

Do not collapse culture/context, provenance, legibility, interaction, interpretation, perceived value, or price.

Q_P L_P I_C J_C V_C P_M

Provenance to legibility: If provenance becomes legible, does anyone interact differently?

Legibility to consumer interaction: If they interact differently, do they interpret the product differently?

Interaction to interpretation: Does interaction alter interpretation?

Interpretation to perceived value: Does interpretation alter perceived value?

Perceived value to price response: Does perceived value affect price response?
